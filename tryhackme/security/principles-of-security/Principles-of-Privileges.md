# Principles of Privileges

Managing access to information systems is important because users should only receive the permissions they need to perform their roles.

The level of access given to a user depends mainly on:

- The user's role or responsibilities within the organisation
- The sensitivity of the information being accessed

## Privileged Identity Management (PIM)

**Privileged Identity Management (PIM)** is used to translate a user's role within an organisation into an appropriate system access role.

### Key idea

> PIM determines what system role a user should have based on their organisational role and responsibilities.

---

## Privileged Access Management (PAM)

**Privileged Access Management (PAM)** manages the privileges and permissions associated with system access roles.

PAM can also include:

- Password management
- Auditing
- Security policies
- Reducing the attack surface

### Key idea

> PAM manages the privileges and permissions associated with system roles.

---

## Principle of Least Privilege

The **principle of least privilege** means that users should receive the minimum permissions necessary to perform their duties.

Giving users unnecessary privileges increases the potential impact of compromised accounts or misuse of permissions.
