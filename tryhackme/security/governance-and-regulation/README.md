&#x20;# Task 2 — Why Is It Important?



&#x20;## Important Terminology



&#x20;### Governance



&#x20;Managing and directing an organisation or system to achieve its objectives while ensuring compliance with applicable laws, regulations, and standards.



&#x20;### Regulation



&#x20;A rule or law enforced by a governing body to ensure compliance and protect against harm.



&#x20;### Compliance



&#x20;The state of adhering to applicable laws, regulations, and standards.



&#x20;## Information Security Governance



&#x20;Information security governance is the structure of policies, processes, methods, and guidelines used to protect an organisation's information assets.



&#x20;It helps organisations:



&#x20;- Manage cybersecurity risks.

\- Protect sensitive information.

\- Define security responsibilities.

\- Meet legal and regulatory requirements.

\- Align security with business objectives.



&#x20;## Processes of Information Security Governance



&#x20;1. \*\*Strategy\*\* — Develop a security strategy aligned with business objectives.

2\. \*\*Policies and procedures\*\* — Establish rules for using and protecting information assets.

3\. \*\*Risk management\*\* — Identify threats and risks and implement mitigation measures.

4\. \*\*Performance measurement\*\* — Use metrics and KPIs to measure security effectiveness.

5\. \*\*Compliance\*\* — Ensure compliance with relevant regulations and industry practices.



&#x20;## Information Security Regulation



&#x20;Information security regulations establish legal or regulatory requirements for protecting information and sensitive data.



&#x20;Examples include:



&#x20;- \*\*GDPR\*\* — Data privacy and protection.

\- \*\*HIPAA\*\* — Healthcare information.

\- \*\*PCI DSS\*\* — Payment-card data security.

\- \*\*PIPEDA\*\* — Privacy and protection of personal information in Canada.

\- \*\*GLBA\*\* — Protection of nonpublic personal information in the US financial sector.



&#x20;## Benefits



&#x20;Effective security governance and compliance can:



&#x20;- Strengthen an organisation's security posture.

\- Protect sensitive information.

\- Increase stakeholder confidence.

\- Help meet legal and regulatory requirements.

\- Align security with business objectives.

\- Support informed security decisions.

\- Demonstrate commitment to protecting sensitive data.

&#x20;\*\*Answer:\*\* `regulation`



&#x20;### Q2. Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) targets which domain for data protection?

&#x20;# Task 3 — Information Security Frameworks



&#x20;An information security framework provides documents and requirements that guide how an organisation's security is implemented, managed, and enforced.



&#x20;## Types of Governance Documents



&#x20;| Document | Purpose |

| --- | --- |

| \*\*Policy\*\* | Defines organisational goals, principles, and rules |

| \*\*Standard\*\* | Establishes specific requirements or specifications |

| \*\*Guideline\*\* | Provides recommendations and best practices |

| \*\*Procedure\*\* | Defines specific steps for performing a task |

| \*\*Baseline\*\* | Defines minimum security requirements |



\### Easy way to remember



&#x20;\*\*Policy → what/why\*\*



&#x20;\*\*Standard → specific requirement\*\*



&#x20;\*\*Guideline → recommendation\*\*



&#x20;\*\*Procedure → how/steps\*\*



&#x20;\*\*Baseline → minimum requirement\*\*



&#x20;## Developing Governance Documents



&#x20;A general process is:



&#x20;1. \*\*Identify scope and purpose\*\*

2\. \*\*Research and review\*\*

3\. \*\*Draft the document\*\*

4\. \*\*Review and obtain approval\*\*

5\. \*\*Implement and communicate\*\*

6\. \*\*Review and update\*\*



&#x20;## Example — Password Policy



&#x20;A password policy can define:



&#x20;- Minimum length and complexity.

\- Password usage requirements.

\- Unique passwords for accounts.

\- Restrictions on password sharing.

\- Secure password storage and transmission.

\- Password reset procedures.

\- Employee communication and training.

\- Compliance monitoring.



&#x20;## Example — Incident Response Procedure



&#x20;An incident response procedure can define:



&#x20;- Types of incidents.

\- Roles and responsibilities.

\- Initial response and containment.

\- Evidence preservation.

\- Investigation and root-cause analysis.

\- Recovery procedures.

\- Reporting and documentation.

\- Communication.

\- Periodic review and updates.



&#x20;Organisations can also adopt existing frameworks and standards rather than creating everything themselves. The appropriate framework depends on factors such as regulatory requirements, geographical location, scope, objectives, and available resources.



&#x20;## Questions



&#x20;### Q1. The step that involves monitoring compliance and adjusting the document based on feedback and changes in the threat landscape or regulatory environment is called?



&#x20;\*\*Answer:\*\* `review and update`



&#x20;### Q2. A set of specific steps for undertaking a particular task or process is called?



&#x20;\*\*Answer:\*\* `procedure`

&#x20;# Task 4 — Governance, Risk Management \& Compliance (GRC)



&#x20;\*\*GRC\*\* is an integrated approach to managing:



&#x20;- \*\*Governance\*\*

\- \*\*Risk Management\*\*

\- \*\*Compliance\*\*



&#x20;## Components of GRC



&#x20;### 1\\. Governance



&#x20;Sets the organisation's direction through:



&#x20;- Security strategy.

\- Policies.

\- Standards.

\- Baselines.

\- Frameworks.

\- Monitoring and performance measurement.



&#x20;### 2\\. Risk Management



&#x20;Involves:



&#x20;- Identifying risks.

\- Assessing risks.

\- Prioritising risks.

\- Implementing controls and mitigation strategies.

\- Monitoring and reporting risks.

\- Continuously improving risk management.



&#x20;### 3\\. Compliance



&#x20;Ensures that an organisation meets:



&#x20;- Legal requirements.

\- Regulatory requirements.

\- Industry obligations.

\- Internal policies and procedures.



&#x20;Compliance can involve audits, assessments, reporting, and compliance programs.



&#x20;## Developing a GRC Program



&#x20;A general GRC process is:



&#x20;\*\*Define scope and objectives → Conduct risk assessment → Develop policies and procedures → Establish governance processes → Implement controls → Monitor and measure → Continuously improve\*\*



&#x20;### Example Controls



&#x20;Technical controls may include:



&#x20;- Firewalls.

\- IDS/IPS.

\- SIEM.

\- Access controls.

\- Logging and monitoring.



&#x20;Non-technical controls can include:



&#x20;- Security awareness training.

\- Policies.

\- Governance committees.

\- Defined roles and responsibilities.



&#x20;## Financial Sector Example



&#x20;Financial organisations may use governance and compliance activities involving:



&#x20;- Financial policies.

\- Anti-money-laundering requirements.

\- Financial audits.

\- Crisis management.

\- PCI DSS.

\- GLBA.



&#x20;Risk management can address:



&#x20;- Financial fraud.

\- Fraudulent transactions.

\- Phishing.

\- Stolen credentials.

\- Fake ATM cards.

&#x20;# Task 5 — Privacy and Data Protection



&#x20;Privacy and data protection regulations are important because organisations in sectors such as finance, healthcare, government, and industry handle \*\*Personally Identifiable Information (PII)\*\*.



&#x20;## GDPR



&#x20;The \*\*General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)\*\* is an EU data protection regulation that protects personal data.



&#x20;Important principles covered in the room include:



&#x20;- Obtain appropriate approval before collecting personal data.

\- Collect only data that is necessary.

\- Protect stored personal data appropriately.

\- Have a legitimate reason for collecting personal data.

\- Inform individuals about how their data is processed.



&#x20;### GDPR Maximum Fine



&#x20;For the most serious infringements, GDPR can impose a maximum fine of:



&#x20;\*\*4% of worldwide annual turnover or €20 million, whichever is higher.\*\*



&#x20;## PCI DSS



&#x20;\*\*Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS)\*\* focuses on protecting payment-card information and securing card transactions.



&#x20;It includes requirements around:



&#x20;- Access control.

\- Monitoring.

\- Protection of cardholder information.

\- Encryption.

\- Security controls such as web application firewalls.



&#x20;### CHD



&#x20;\*\*CHD = Cardholder Data\*\*



&#x20;## PIPEDA



&#x20;\*\*PIPEDA\*\* is Canada's federal private-sector privacy law. It concerns how organisations collect, use, and disclose personal information in commercial activities.



&#x20;## Questions

&#x20;### Q2. In terms of PCI DSS, what does CHD stand for?

&#x20;# Task 6 — NIST Special Publications



&#x20;## NIST SP 800-53



&#x20;\*\*NIST Special Publication 800-53 — Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organisations\*\* provides a catalogue of security and privacy controls.



&#x20;It helps organisations:



&#x20;- Protect information systems.

\- Protect organisational assets and personnel.

\- Address security and privacy risks.

\- Improve their security posture.

\- Support compliance with applicable requirements.



&#x20;Revision 5 organises controls into \*\*20 control families\*\*.



&#x20;### Program Management



&#x20;Program Management is an important control family for establishing, implementing, and monitoring organisation-wide information security and privacy programs.



&#x20;## NIST 800-53 Compliance Practices



&#x20;A general approach includes:



&#x20;1. Discover and catalogue assets, systems, and threats.

2\. \*\*Map\*\* controls to identified assets and risks.

3\. Establish governance and assign responsibilities.

4\. Implement and maintain controls.

5\. Monitor and evaluate controls.

6\. Conduct assessments and audits.

7\. Continuously improve the implementation.



&#x20;## NIST SP 800-63B



&#x20;NIST SP 800-63B provides guidelines for \*\*digital identity and authentication\*\*.



&#x20;It covers areas such as:



&#x20;- Identity assurance.

\- Authentication.

\- Passwords.

\- Biometrics.

\- Tokens.

\- Credential management.

&#x20;# Task 7 — Information Security Management and Compliance



&#x20;Information Security Management involves planning, implementing, and continuously managing security measures to protect information assets.



&#x20;It includes:



&#x20;- Risk assessment.

\- Security controls.

\- Security procedures.

\- Incident response.

\- Security awareness training.



&#x20;Compliance involves following applicable:



&#x20;- Laws.

\- Regulations.

\- Contracts.

\- Industry standards.



&#x20;## ISO/IEC 27001



&#x20;\*\*ISO/IEC 27001\*\* is an international standard for establishing and maintaining an \*\*Information Security Management System (ISMS)\*\*.



&#x20;### Core Components



&#x20;- \*\*Scope\*\* — Defines the boundaries of the ISMS.

\- \*\*Information security policy\*\* — Defines the organisation's security direction.

\- \*\*Risk assessment\*\* — Identifies and evaluates security risks.

\- \*\*Risk treatment\*\* — Selects and implements controls to reduce risks.

\- \*\*Statement of Applicability (SoA)\*\* — Identifies applicable controls and their applicability.

\- \*\*Internal audit\*\* — Evaluates whether the ISMS operates effectively.

\- \*\*Management review\*\* — Reviews ISMS performance.



&#x20;### ISO 27001 Implementation



&#x20;An effective ISMS requires:



&#x20;- Leadership support.

\- Resource allocation.

\- Risk assessment.

\- Appropriate security controls.

\- Monitoring and measurement.

\- Regular review.

\- Continual improvement.



&#x20;## SOC 2



&#x20;\*\*SOC 2\*\* is an auditing/attestation framework developed by the \*\*American Institute of Certified Public Accountants (AICPA)\*\*.



&#x20;It evaluates controls relevant to service organisations that handle customer information.



&#x20;Independent auditors assess the relevant controls within the defined scope.



&#x20;Audits can examine areas such as:



&#x20;- Physical security.

\- Network security.

\- Data encryption.

\- Backup and recovery.

\- Employee training.

\- Incident management.



&#x20;### SOC 2 Audit Process



&#x20;A general process is:



&#x20;1. Determine the scope.

2\. Choose a suitable auditor.

3\. Plan the audit.

4\. Prepare policies, procedures, and controls.

5\. Conduct the audit and testing.

6\. Receive the audit report.

7\. Address identified deficiencies where applicable.



&#x20;The controls examined depend on the \*\*scope of the audit\*\*.

&#x20;# Key Takeaways



&#x20;- \*\*Governance\*\* establishes direction, policies, responsibilities, and oversight.

\- \*\*Regulations\*\* establish legal or regulatory requirements.

\- \*\*Compliance\*\* means adhering to applicable requirements.

\- \*\*GRC\*\* combines governance, risk management, and compliance.

\- \*\*Policies\*\* define organisational rules and direction.

\- \*\*Standards\*\* define specific requirements.

\- \*\*Guidelines\*\* provide recommendations.

\- \*\*Procedures\*\* define specific steps.

\- \*\*Baselines\*\* define minimum security requirements.

\- \*\*GDPR\*\* focuses on personal-data protection.

\- \*\*PCI DSS\*\* focuses on payment-card data security.

\- \*\*NIST SP 800-53\*\* provides security and privacy controls.

\- \*\*NIST SP 800-63B\*\* focuses on digital identity and authentication.
